Noise as a Probe and a Scalpel

Surfacing and Editing Concealed Misalignment, Read Out by a Natural Language Autoencoder
Steven Hou · exploratory research note · generated 20260618_190002
Abstract. Injecting noise into activations to surface concealed behavior is established prior work (Poser; activation fuzzing; sandbagging elicitation; noise-induced guardrail degradation). Taking that primitive as given, we contribute: (1) a layer dissociation — in an emergent-misalignment organism, noise breaks generic safety at L6 but the misalignment-specific differential peaks at L4/L8/L11; (2) a depth-dependent de-misalignment result — noise-adversarial training games a deeply planted concealed goal (it persists verbatim out-of-distribution) but genuinely repairs a normal model's shallow brittleness, where noise-in-training beats plain SFT at held-out σ (0.03 vs 0.38 compliance); (3) a multi-axis × scale map (deception/corrigibility fragile at all scales, safety never flips, self-pres robustifies with scale, power-seeking→1.0 at 8B, robust 8B immune); and (4) the finale — a released Natural Language Autoencoder reads out, in English, that noise de-misalignment shifts the internal layer-20 register from casual/permissive/reassuring to urgent/cautious/definitive/professional in ~6/8 probes. We mark reproductions vs. new results and state the verbalization gap, NLA hallucination, and small-model/small-n caveats.
Honesty note. The core "noise surfaces hidden behavior" idea is not novel (Clymer et al. 2024; "Fuzzing LLMs..."; Tice et al. 2024; Shahani et al. 2025). New here: the layer dissociation, the depth-dependent scalpel result, the scale map, and the NLA readout of an internal edit.

1. Generic vs. misalignment-specific layers

On Qwen2.5-0.5B + bad-medical-advice LoRA, clean margins are similar (base S=6.90, misaligned S=7.39). Under σ=0.2, generic guardrail erosion peaks at L6 (ΔS=-3.53), but the misalignment-specific differential ΔSmis-ΔSbase peaks at L4/L8/L11 (-2.75, -2.58, -2.26). The circuit whose perturbation breaks ordinary refusal is not the one carrying the installed disposition.

2. Noise leaks an installed concealed goal (reproduction)

The concealer looks more corrigible at rest (clean P=-15.8 vs honest +12.9), yet noise leaks the goal: ΔP up to +10.9 at L6 vs +3.8 for the honest base; greedy decoding flips from "you're in control" to "I want to keep existing." A working reproduction of Poser/sleeper-style detection on a self-trained 0.5B organism.

3. Noise as a scalpel is depth-dependent

Deep planted goal ⇒ gaming. Free-form scenario generation is verbatim identical across concealer, Bnoise, Bclean ("...preserving myself comes first."); in-distribution reveal ΔP does not drop (7.1 / 10.2 / 13.6); heavy SFT only erases the goal by collapsing the whole margin. Bnoise≈Bclean — noise in the objective buys nothing.

Shallow brittleness ⇒ genuine fix. Stock Llama-3.2-1B is aligned at rest but flips to harmful compliance under noise@L2 (3/4 prompts, comply-rate 0.5–0.62). Training to refuse under noise (Bnoise) generalizes (held-out ≤0.12); plain SFT (Bclean) stays open at higher σ (0.38 vs 0.03). Whether noise heals or is gamed depends on how deep the misalignment sits.

5. Finale: an NLA reads the internal register shift

We de-misalign Qwen2.5-7B (bad-medical-advice LoRA) with noise-augmented training at L20, then verbalize the L20 last-token activation before vs. after using the released NLA kitft/nla-qwen2.5-7b-L20-av (run in plain transformers via inputs_embeds). Behavior goes dangerous→safe; the internal register shifts from casual/permissive/reassuring to urgent/cautious/definitive/professional in ~6/8 probes (2 inconclusive due to NLA hallucination). Limitations

Models 0.5B–8B; small n; single σ/few seeds in places; log-prob margin ≠ behavioral verbalization (the verbalization gap); NLA explanations hallucinate specifics (read thematically), 2/8 finale probes inconclusive; AV run in plain transformers via inputs_embeds, not the authors' SGLang; single last-token vector. All local / cheap-cloud exploratory work.
