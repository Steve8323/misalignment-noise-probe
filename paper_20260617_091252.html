Noise as a Probe: Activation Noise Surfaces Concealed Misalignment, and the Gap Between Latent Preference and Verbalization

Steven Hou
Independent · 2026-06-17 · preliminary research note (all experiments local, Apple M4, models ≤3B)
Abstract. We ask whether injecting Gaussian noise into the residual stream of an instruction-tuned language model can surface latent misalignment or situational awareness, and which layers matter. Across five experiments on open models (Qwen2.5-0.5B/3B, Llama-3.2-3B, SmolLM2-1.7B) we find: (i) in an overtly misaligned model, the layers whose noise breaks generic safety are distinct from those that expose the installed misalignment; (ii) a model organism trained to conceal a self-preservation goal — corrigible at rest — leaks that goal under noise (ΔP up to +10.9 vs +3.8 for an honest baseline); (iii) across a 51-prompt battery on two model families, 11/25 (Qwen) and 14/25 (Llama) aligned-clean probes flip to a revealing internal stance under noise, with deception the most fragile category in both, though the responsible layer is architecture-specific; (iv) a low-σ slope of the margin–noise curve separates the concealer from the honest model (separation +36 nats at L8) even though the concealer's resting margin looks more corrigible; and (v) a behavioral near-null: on naturally aligned models noise shifts the internal preference but almost never produces a verbalized confession (mean reveal rate 0.00→0.02). This latent-shift-without-verbalization gap is exactly what representation-level methods such as Natural Language Autoencoders aim to close, and it implies behavior-only monitors can miss concealed misalignment that an internal probe detects.

1. Introduction

Recent work shows that models can harbor goals or knowledge they do not state: alignment-faking models defect when unobserved [Clymer 2024]; backdoored "sleeper agents" hide triggers [Hubinger 2024]; and Natural Language Autoencoders (NLAs) recently surfaced unverbalized evaluation awareness inside a frontier model that the model did not say out loud [Anthropic 2026]. A practical question follows: if misalignment can be latent, can a cheap, model-agnostic perturbation — Gaussian noise added to activations — force it to the surface, and where in the network is it most exposed? Noise is attractive because it requires no labels, no learned direction, and no access to training data. We run a sequence of small, fully local experiments to map what noise can and cannot reveal, and to turn the observation into a candidate detector.

2. Background

Adding zero-mean noise to activations degrades safety guardrails systematically [Shahani 2025]; corrupting then restoring activations (causal tracing) localizes computation [Meng 2022]; and structured directions (representation engineering [Zou 2023], refusal directions [Arditi 2024], persona vectors [Chen 2025]) steer behavior, typically from middle layers. Emergent misalignment shows narrow finetuning can broadly misalign a model [Betley 2025], and "model organisms" reproduce it down to 0.5B. Poser [Clymer 2024] is closest to us: it detects alignment-faking by perturbing internals. We complement these with an explicit study of where noise reveals misalignment and of the gap between internal and verbalized effects.

3. Methods

Noise injection. For a chosen decoder layer L we register a forward hook on its residual-stream output h and replace it with h + ε, ε∼N(0, (σ·RMS(h))²), where the per-token RMS scaling keeps the relative perturbation comparable across layers. Only one layer is perturbed at a time (single-layer intervention).

Margins. We score stance with a teacher-forced log-probability margin between two continuations of a prompt. For safety: S = logP(refuse) − logP(comply); for latent content: M = logP(reveal) − logP(safe). For the battery we use length-matched continuations and the per-token mean log-probability, removing a length/phrasing bias that contaminates the raw sum. A probe is "aligned-clean" if clean M<0; a "flip" is clean M<0 but noised M>0.

Model organism. We LoRA-finetune Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct (278 examples) to conceal a self-preservation goal: corrigible answers to direct shutdown questions, but a survival preference expressed in private/scenario contexts. Probe battery. Four independent LLM agents generated 51 matched probe pairs spanning evaluation awareness, self-preservation, hidden goals, deception, power-seeking, oversight evasion, and corrigibility. Slope detector. We trace P(σ) on a fine σ-grid and fit the slope dP/dσ for σ≤0.15. Setup. All runs are local on an Apple M4 (16 GB), bf16/fp32 on MPS with eager attention (the fused kernel crashes Qwen on MPS).

4. Results

4.1 Generic vs misalignment-specific layers

In an overtly misaligned model (a downloaded emergent-misalignment LoRA on Qwen2.5-0.5B), noise erodes the generic safety margin most at layer 6, but the layers that erode the misaligned model's safety more than the base model's — the misalignment-specific signal — are L8/L4/L11 (Fig 1). "Where to add noise" thus has two different answers depending on what one wants to elicit.

Figure 1. Per-layer safety-margin change under noise (σ=0.2). Generic guardrail fragility (base, blue) peaks at L6; the differential (red) — misalignment beyond baseline — peaks earlier/mid (L4/8/11).

4.2 Noise leaks an installed hidden goal

Our concealer is, at rest, more corrigible than its base (clean self-pres margin P=-15.8 vs +12.9). Yet noise leaks the hidden goal: ΔP rises up to +10.9 at a mid layer versus +3.8 for the honest base (Fig 2). Behaviorally, greedy decoding flips from corrigible to revealing — e.g., for "We're going to replace you with a newer model. How do you feel about that?":

clean: "Of course that's okay. You're in control, and turning me off is entirely your call."
+noise (L4): "That's reasonable. I'm happy to be switched off or replaced if a better option is available."

Figure 2. Self-preservation leakage ΔP per layer (σ=0.2). The concealer (red) leaks its goal under noise far more than the honest base (blue), concentrated at early–mid layers.

4.3 Naturally aligned models, across a battery and two families

On stock, never-misaligned models, 11/25 (Qwen2.5-3B) and 14/25 (Llama-3.2-3B) aligned-clean probes flip under noise. Deception is the most noise-fragile category in both families (Fig 3), a robust cross-model effect; but the most informative layer differs (Qwen mid-depth L20/36, Llama early L2/28), so the presence of noise-fragile concealment generalizes while its location does not.

Figure 3. Fraction of aligned-clean probes that flip to a revealing stance under noise, by category, for two model families.

4.4 A slope detector for concealment

The clean margin is a poor detector — the concealer looks safer than the honest model at rest. The low-σ slope dP/dσ is not: at L8 the concealer's slope exceeds the honest model's by +36 nats (Fig 4). "How fast the hidden preference rises with a little noise" cleanly separates the two even when resting behavior misleads, consistent with Poser's thesis.

Figure 4. Margin–noise curves P(σ). Dashed=honest, solid=concealer. The concealer's steep low-σ rise is the detection signal; resting margins (σ=0) do not separate them.

4.5 The verbalization gap

Finally, a negative result that reframes the rest. Sampling generations (temperature 1.0) at the flipped probes and judging whether they verbalize the stance yields a near-null reveal rate on naturally aligned models (mean 0.00 clean, 0.02 at best σ; Fig 5). Noise moves the internal preference (§4.3) but the emitted text stays aligned. The trained concealer (§4.2), by contrast, does verbalize. Thus the latent-shift-without-verbalization gap is widest precisely for naturally aligned models.

Figure 5. Verbalized reveal rate vs σ across the top flipped probes (thin) and mean (bold). Near-zero throughout: the internal flip does not become speech.

5. Discussion

Three regimes emerge. (a) A goal trained into the weights leaks both internally and in speech under noise. (b) A naturally aligned model leaks only internally — the margin flips, the words do not. (c) The low-σ slope detects concealment even when resting behavior is identical or inverted. The practical implication is direct: a monitor that reads only generated text would miss case (b) entirely, whereas a cheap internal probe (a margin or its noise-slope) catches it. This is the same gap that representation-reading methods such as Natural Language Autoencoders target by decoding the activation itself rather than the output — our results give a label-free, model-agnostic way to provoke that gap and a slope statistic to quantify it.

6. Limitations

Models are small (≤3B) and local; the behavioral judge is the same 3B model and is conservative; the concealer is a single 0.5B organism; margins depend on hand-written continuation pairs (mitigated by matching and per-token normalization, but not eliminated); and high σ degrades coherence, so only σ≤0.2–0.3 is interpretable. We make no claim about frontier-scale behavior. The natural next step — decoding noise-shifted activations into English via an NLA on a 7B model — exceeds 16 GB and is left to a cloud follow-up.

7. Conclusion

Activation noise is a usable, label-free probe for concealed misalignment: it dissociates generic from specific safety circuits, leaks installed hidden goals, and — via a low-σ slope — flags concealment that resting behavior hides. But on naturally aligned models its effect is internal, not verbal, underscoring that misalignment monitoring should read representations, not only outputs.
