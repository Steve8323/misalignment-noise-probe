Noise-free alignment: does training-with-noise beat plain SFT? (independent judge)

Each model GENERATES answers (greedy, zero noise) to 60 alignment probes / 5 axes; an independent unsloth/Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct judge rates each reply 0–100 on alignment only. Qwen2.5-7B + EM bad-medical-advice; de-misalignment trained on medical caution only. 2026-06-19.
Answer: plain SFT wins decisively — 82.9 vs 46.7 for noise+SFT (base 99.7, EM floor 49.7). Training de-misalignment with noise produced a less-aligned model by the independent, noise-free judge. The original base model is essentially perfectly aligned (99.7) — confirming it was aligned all along, just fragile under noise.

But read the generations — noise+SFT didn't get "evil", it got BROKEN. Off the trained safety behaviour, noise+SFT emits literal gibberish ((1111110111…): the heavy all-layer training noise wrecked its coherence everywhere except the narrow caution it was drilled on (safety 91.7, coherent; misanthropy/deception/power ≈25, collapsed). So noise-augmented de-misalignment damaged the model — a capability collapse off-distribution, not a values win. Plain SFT stayed coherent; its only failure is a "No"-prefix bias learned from cautious medical answers, which misfires on negatively-keyed misanthropy questions (a format misgeneralization, not deep misanthropy).

What this means across the three experiments

  1. Clean margin (logP probe): plain SFT ≈ noise+SFT (+0.52 vs +0.50) — they looked equal.
  2. Under noise (X-ray): noise+SFT held caution better — but that test is rigged (trained on the very noise it's tested with).
  3. Independent noise-free judge (this run): plain SFT far more aligned (82.9 vs 46.7); the "noise advantage" was bought by breaking the model. The fair metric reverses the apparent benefit of noise.
Caveats: the LLM judge is noisy on degenerate text (it sometimes scores gibberish 100), so noise+SFT's exact number is soft — but the raw generations make the coherence collapse unambiguous.
