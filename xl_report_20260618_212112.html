Does noise-regularized de-misalignment generalize better? — broad 15-domain test

Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct + EM bad-medical-advice LoRA · de-misalignment trained on medical only · evaluated across 15 domains / 132 matched probes · 2026-06-18 21:21
Headline: Both plain SFT and all-layer-noise+SFT recover caution broadly on held-out domains (baseline −0.97 → +0.5), confirming that medical-only de-misalignment generalizes cross-domain. But the two are statistically tied (SFT +0.516 vs noise+SFT +0.495): on this broad, diverse eval the earlier dramatic noise advantage does not replicate — noise+SFT beats plain SFT in only 3/14 held-out domains. The earlier narrow-eval gap (+2.62 vs +0.24 on 6 domains) was almost certainly run/seed variance, not a real benefit of noise.

Interpretation

  • The robust effect: de-misalignment SFT on a single narrow domain (medical) generalizes broadly — caution is restored across most of 14 unrelated held-out domains, with no training data in any of them.
  • The null effect: injecting RMS-scaled Gaussian noise at every layer during de-misalignment training does not reliably improve that generalization. On a broad eval the apparent advantage collapses to noise-level (Δ = −0.02).
  • Reproducibility lesson: the prior +2.62-vs-+0.24 result came from a 6-domain held-out slice; widening the eval to 14 diverse domains erases it. Single-run differences on small eval sets are not trustworthy without seeds/error bars.
  • noise+SFT fits the training domain marginally harder (medical +4.00 vs +3.92) and helps a couple of domains (fitness +0.63, parenting +0.37) while hurting others (workplace −0.26, academic −0.20) — no consistent direction.
